Recent advances in artificial intelligence for partial differential equation (PDE) solving (AI4PDEs) have been driven by pioneering architectures such as Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), which offer new possibilities through trainable activation functions and strong expressive capabilities. However, existing methods face persistent challenges: gradient-based PINNs and KANs incur prohibitive training costs and are prone to local minima, while Physics-Informed Extreme Learning Machines (PIELMs) exhibit limited robustness on complex problems. Recent work such as HKAN has adopted randomized learning paradigms with fixed hidden-layer parameters to circumvent backpropagation, yet these efforts focus primarily on generic regression tasks and lack systematic architectural designs tailored to the physics-constraint satisfaction inherent in PDE solving.
To address these gaps, we propose the Extensible Least-Squares Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (ELSKAN), a framework specifically designed for Physics-Informed PDE solving. ELSKAN integrates Chebyshev-based KAN representations with the ELM training philosophy, unifying PDE residuals, boundary conditions, and initial conditions into a single regularized least-squares problem through global coupled matrix assembly, thereby enabling gradient-free rapid solving. Building on this foundation, we introduce three problem-driven architectural variants. First, S-LSKAN employs a dual-branch high-low frequency fusion strategy for multi-frequency coupled problems. Second, H-LSKAN leverages heterogeneous local bases combining Diff-Sigmoid and Gaussian RBF kernels for localized structures such as Gaussian peaks. Third, DP-LSKAN utilizes domain decomposition with cPINN-style interface conditions and hard-constraint exclusion strategies for crack-tip singularities and discontinuities.
Validation on eight PDE benchmarks, including wave propagation, Helmholtz equations, and Mode-III fracture problems, demonstrates that ELSKAN achieves up to two orders of magnitude higher accuracy than PIKAN and PIELM with fewer parameters, while reducing training time by one to two orders of magnitude. These gains stem from direct linearized solving of Physics-Informed constraints, which avoids the numerical instabilities of iterative optimization, and problem-driven basis-function matching, which aligns architectural design with the intrinsic physical structure of PDE solutions (multi-frequency, localized, discontinuous). The framework is further enhanced by a frequency-aware initialization algorithm and a Newton-ELSKAN extension for nonlinear problems, broadening its practical applicability.
